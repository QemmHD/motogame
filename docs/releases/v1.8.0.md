# v1.8.0 — Crash Theater

Release-candidate date: **2026-07-14**

Moto Rush X3 v1.8 turns a failed run into a short, readable stunt story without changing how the failed run is judged. The original **Splitline** crash model separates the bike and rider into 17 articulated parts, selects one of ten authored cause profiles, collides against a detached snapshot of terrain and frozen platform tops, and converts bounded contact records into deterministic secondary feedback. A cause card keeps retry timing clear on desktop and mobile, while Reduced Motion preserves the result with a static projected pose and no zoom, slow motion, hitstop, flash, shake, kick, or impact vibration.

This is an **unmerged release candidate**, not a live deployment. The complete local npm gate, four deterministic browser captures, and draft-PR hosted gate recorded below pass at gameplay SHA `89e5f7d9757e`. The physical-device matrix and production Pages/cache/install/offline smoke remain explicitly `pending`.

## Player-visible update

### Splitline bike-and-rider model

The Crash Theater rig has 17 simulated parts:

| Group | Parts |
|---|---|
| Detached bike | rear wheel, front wheel, frame, seat, handlebar |
| Rider core | hip, torso, head, helmet |
| Segmented arms | rear elbow, front elbow, rear hand, front hand |
| Segmented legs | rear knee, front knee, rear foot, front foot |

Canvas primitives draw the model at runtime. The far side uses deeper cobalt/obsidian values; the near side uses orange armor, amber elbow/knee guards, and cyan reflective seams; the helmet has a separate amber shell, face area, and smoke-blue visor. The detached bike adds a frame triangle, tank/shrouds, seat, fork, swingarm, engine center, rims, spokes, and tires around the same simulated points. This is a code-native model, not a competitor-derived sprite or a new bitmap payload.

Structural constraints preserve a readable skeleton. Five softer rider-to-bike tethers can break under strain or be released by a cause profile, allowing hand, foot, seat, or complete separation without random forces. The pose still uses hard speed, distance, correction, step, solver, and lifetime limits.

### Ten cause profiles

Crash reasons are normalized into detached immutable presentation records. Each profile owns an original card label, accent, glyph, separate rider/bike impulse balance, and tether-release instruction:

| Cause ID | Card label | Presentation direction |
|---|---|---|
| `collision` | **AXLE BREAK** | Balanced rider/bike separation with all tethers initially available. |
| `terrain` | **DIRT BITE** | Lower dirt-impact beat with a restrained peel-away. |
| `platform` | **DECK SLAM** | Sharper steel-deck lift and cyan card accent. |
| `saw` | **SAW KISS** | Faster rider spin with both hand tethers released. |
| `spikes` | **SPIKE CHECK** | Upward rider impulse with both foot tethers released. |
| `barrel` | **DRUM BLAST** | Strong radial separation with all rider-to-bike tethers released. |
| `mace` | **CHAIN HAMMER** | Lateral hammer response with both hand tethers released. |
| `crusher` | **PRESS LOCK** | Compact low-energy separation with all tethers released. |
| `tnt` | **REDLINE BURST** | Strongest rider/bike blast split with complete tether release. |
| `fallout` | **VOID DROP** | Restrained drop response with both foot tethers released. |

Aliases such as `ground`, `deck`, `blade`, `drum`, `press`, and `void` map to the intended profile. Unknown, cyclic, non-finite, or otherwise malformed inputs fall back to `collision`, remain finite, and cannot mutate their source.

Terrain and one-way-platform head strikes now create their natural detached presentation reason during ordinary physics/session play, so **DIRT BITE** and **DECK SLAM** are not development-hook-only cards. The event copies can be annotated by the browser without changing the source. This contact metadata is intentionally excluded from authoritative `lastCrash` and proof snapshots; the v1.7 Gold state hashes therefore remain intact.

### Impact beat and cause card

Crash entry now produces a short cause-colored glyph burst, then blends from the authoritative bike pose into the detached Splitline rig. The camera measures a bounded pose that includes bike and rider, frames the complete separation, and adds a quickly decaying seeded roll, kick, shake, flash, hitstop, and slow-motion beat. The overlay reports the authored cause label plus current part count, unique touchpoint count, and peak impact, and retains the existing tap/primary-action checkpoint retry.

Entry debris and later ragdoll-contact particles use stable hash lanes derived from build, level, run tick, cause, impact tick, node, and effect lane. Identical staged crashes therefore receive identical presentation variation. These seeds are never read by the authoritative run session or replay proof.

Secondary contact feedback is bounded in three places:

- the ragdoll queues at most **48** pending impact records and counts any overflow;
- the browser consumes at most four records from one presentation batch;
- a crash emits at most **28** secondary contact particles, while impact audio/haptics are throttled to readable intervals.

The existing global effect pools remain capped at 384 particles, 32 popups, and 220 tracks.

## Detached crash-contact field

`public/crash-contact.js` creates an independent field when the run enters the crashed state. It clones valid terrain segments, enabled flags, surface metadata, and the current rectangle of each platform. Moving decks are frozen at their crash-entry pose for the life of the presentation. The public field and platform records are frozen; terrain query scratch state lives privately and does not alias authoritative segment arrays, buckets, `enabled`, `seen`, or `seenToken` data.

Terrain contact retains source segment identity. Platform contact remains one-way from above, but it continuously sweeps each ragdoll circle from `oldX`/`oldY` to its current position. The solver checks the deck line and both rounded top endpoints, preventing a small fast hand, elbow, knee, or wheel from tunneling through a thin deck while allowing stationary or upward-moving parts underneath to pass through. Surface-specific friction is applied once on the first contact of a physical substep, not once per constraint iteration.

Iteration is hard-bounded to 8,192 terrain segments and 512 platform records, with strict finite coordinate, dimension, and radius limits. Invalid fields or queries fail closed.

## Deterministic ragdoll and impact queue

The crash simulation remains DOM-free and advances at fixed `1 / 120` second substeps with a bounded per-call step count. `readRagdoll()` can reuse renderer buffers while still returning detached data to ordinary callers. Its snapshot now includes total/pending/dropped impact counts, peak and last impact, contacts per node, broken tethers, speed/clamp metrics, nodes, and active constraints.

An impact record is emitted only on a node's first contact in a substep and only when incoming normal speed reaches the configured threshold. Records contain a fixed tick, node and group, position, normal, speed, normalized strength, surface, contact kind, and terrain/platform identity. `drainRagdollImpacts()` copies records into caller-owned storage and clears the internal queue; changing the drained copy cannot change ragdoll state.

The development collision overlay consumes a separately detached and bounded proxy. It can draw current node circles, previous-to-current sweeps, resolved structural/tether links, a complete pose bound, contact/impact totals, and last-impact metadata. Proxy construction does not run contact queries or advance the ragdoll.

## Reduced Motion contract

Reduced Motion is an alternate presentation, not a missing presentation. At crash creation it builds a readable already-fallen Splitline pose, repeatedly projects every part out of detached terrain/platform contact, and does not start dynamic simulation. If Reduced Motion is enabled during a crash, the current pose is frozen and its node histories are neutralized.

For every crash age and cause, the Reduced Motion camera policy guarantees:

- zoom `1` and constant base view height;
- roll, kick, shake, hitstop, and flash `0`;
- slow-motion multiplier `1`;
- no animated impact glyph, secondary impact particles, impact audio, or impact vibration;
- preserved cause card, 17-part silhouette, contact-readable framing, and unchanged retry timing.

The Kinetic Loom renderer continues to freeze—not remove—its directional phase under the same setting.

## Authority and retry boundary

Crash Theater starts only after `run-session.js` has entered an authoritative crash. The session gives the browser a detached copy of the crash reason, preventing presentation annotations from reaching `lastCrash` or the proof snapshot. Ragdoll contact, cause impulses, seeds, camera, particles, audio, haptics, debug proxies, and pose metrics never write to the authoritative bike, rules, hazards, platforms, checkpoints, score, elapsed time, replay tick, or finish proof.

The failure cadence intentionally does **not** change in v1.8:

- manual retry is still consumed and recorded on a fixed input tick;
- automatic retry still reaches the same deterministic boundary from `RUN_SESSION_CRASH_DURATION = 1.85` seconds;
- retry restores the same checkpoint, hazard timeline, activated-platform snapshot, bike state, and stateless Kinetic Loom behavior;
- full level restart still rebuilds authored tick-zero state.

This keeps U05 presentation work from silently crossing into the U06 rules/retry contract.

## Originality boundary

Crash Theater uses broad motorcycle-stunt conventions—rider ejection, detached machinery, impact framing, and fast retry—but does not decompile another game or copy proprietary code, models, art, sounds, UI, tracks, timing, or tuned data. Splitline's 17-part structure, color language, cause names, constraint/impact contracts, contact snapshot, seeded feedback, Canvas rendering, and documentation are authored for Moto Rush X3.
